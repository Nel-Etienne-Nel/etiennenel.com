AI is great, the day-to-day benefits cannot be disputed.

  
Only when used in the right way.

It works well when you need to gather information or put different sources together to build a report. The nature of what makes an LLM so useful is the language aspect, the fact that it understands language so well is why it's so popular today. A machine being able to understand normal conversation is revolutionary.

These advantages do come with a risk however.

As we continue to use it in our day-to-day lives, in more aspects we grow more dependent as a result. You start out by gathering information and getting all of your sources onto one page. It doesn't write in your natural way and you are fine with that, you just needed to get out the report anyway.

The next day comes and you need to do the same thing, you repeat the process even faster and think "Wow, this is great, I feel so productive". But do you really know what that report says? Sure a super smart LLM wrote it so it must be right, right?  
AI writes super well, but every word is a statistical assumption made. AI guesses the next word on what it thinks should be correct. So yes, this report might look amazing, but when you drill down into the information, what use is it really? I'm not saying all AI output is wrong, but the potential for something to look right but have small factual errors are big.  
If it gets one thing wrong early on, it keeps building on that mistake, and slowly but surely it drifts somewhere you never intended.  

![One wrong fact, built on, keeps growing. Each step builds on the last output, so the gap from what you meant compounds.](/images/editorial/002-drift.png)

While going on with your work day by day, week by week and month by month, AI now writes all of your documents, feedback and emails. By now you have hopefully tuned to sound more like you or it's learned your tone of voice a little better. But it still looks artificial in most cases...

After becoming reliant on using AI for everything, you don't read the email and have AI read it for you, and ask it to summarise it. You feed an AI-generated report into AI to help you understand, or draft feedback... In effect, you now have AI talking to AI..

If AI is reading your reports, and writing them..  
What good are you for?

There's a statistic online that said AI-generated writing now matches the amount of human writing published online, which I find incredibly worrisome.

According to Graphite, an SEO agency, [found](https://graphite.io/five-percent/more-articles-are-now-created-by-ai-than-humans) that AI-written articles outnumbered human-written ones by November 2024. Their [updated study](https://gptzero.me/news/ai-written-web/) states that about half of new articles online are now AI-generated.

In my work day I receive and look at so much AI generated material it's actually effort to read. And I know I'm not alone in this, my colleagues grow weary of clients sending AI generated feedback, paragraphs and paragraphs of uniform text that could have been one point.

AI generated text is so uniform in format, from paragraphs of 2 line sentences that look the same, with the fall of em-dashes it's harder to spot, but still extremely evident. Human written emails get straight to the point, simple one line sentences saying what needs to be said, because we humans have a natural tendency to be a bit lazy in writing (might just be me personally, but from what I've seen, I'm confident in my statement). Compare this to AI and your email is double as long and almost over-explains a point. A big difference getting there is also in writing the email, I'd say 80% of the time writing the email by hand is actually faster. Trying to get Claude to write a simple email can be tedious work because it doesn't always have enough context.

An example:

Say a client needs to move Thursday's meeting. Here's the email AI writes:

> Hi Sarah,
> 
> I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out regarding our meeting scheduled for this Thursday.
> 
> Unfortunately, due to some unforeseen scheduling conflicts on my end, I will no longer be able to attend at the originally planned time — and I sincerely apologise for any inconvenience this may cause.
> 
> Would it be possible to reschedule to a time that works for both of us? I'm happy to be flexible — please let me know what suits your availability.
> 
> Looking forward to hearing from you.
> 
> Kind regards, Etienne

And here's the one a person writes:

> Hi Sarah, Unfortunately Thursday doesn't work for me anymore as something has come up. Can we do Friday at 10?
> 
> Kind regards
> 
> Etienne

Yes, email etiquette exists, but you get the idea.

The answer to my earlier question of what good are you for is simple.

![You have the bigger picture. AI only sees the small square you hand it.](/images/editorial/002-bigger-picture.png)

You hold the important client context in your head, you know what matters and what doesn't, AI doesn't. Having AI write most of your things can be an accelerant if used right, otherwise it's a crutch.

In my experience and depending on how well your AI system is set up, 90% of the time it does not have the context it needs to do anything properly or right the first time.

In order to prevent the drift I mentioned earlier, you need to do your part. Meaning  
  
1. Give enough context:

Show the complete picture, what you have, what you want to do, what right looks like.

2. After the first output, make absolutely sure every fact and claim is correct.

When you are using AI the right way, you get work done faster, more accurate. All of this can happen if you stop outsourcing your part.

AI should do the work, not the thinking.
