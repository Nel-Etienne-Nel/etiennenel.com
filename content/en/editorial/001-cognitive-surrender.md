## If AI can give you the answer, what is thinking actually for?

Cognitive surrender, in my view is the art of surrendering some but not all of your thinking to an external source. Be it this asking your friend for their input, running with that and using it without thinking it over or what it's more relevant to today where everyone is offloading their thinking to AI.

This is a major issue.

Students are using AI to do their assignments for them, which leads to them without the critical skills they need to form skills they need for the job market be this actual skills or stretching your thinking ability.

With today's technology you have millions of articles of information at your fingertips, you are supposed to be smarter than ever.. yet I'd argue we are the dumbest we have ever been. Our brains need to work in order to stay "smart" and if it doesn't, it discards brain cells (Don't quote me on this, I didn't have biology and don't use AI to write my articles.)

### How does this look practically?

In building with AI or "vibe coding" you often start with an idea and off you go building. You monitor progress and give active input up until a point. Then the tool you use suggests some features and you think "Sure, that sounds awesome" and AI implements them. At this stage you are not keeping track of what it's building or how. What had started off as an easy and quick project evolved into a monster that, if it actually works, you have no idea how it works underneath the hood. It becomes a working black box essentially. This is the problem with this process when you just accept what the AI feeds you... this is how you practice cognitive surrender.

Software engineers are shipping code they haven't read, which leads to vulnerabilities and untrusted software, sure it works, will it after you change something later? And can you change something later on your own if you don't have access to AI?

I read a book that also illustrates this in a different way.

In the book *Thinking, Fast and Slow* the author mentions system 1 & 2 thinking. Where system 1 is fast automatic and instinctive. No effort is required from your side. And system 2 is on the other end of the road. This is slow, deliberate, effortful. What you would use to work through a difficult problem.

Your natural inclination is to use system 1 more because it's less effort, and for a lot of times it's useful, but for when you need to build something or think something over you need system 2.

People who let AI do all their work for them are slaves to their System 1 thoughts.

The author also argues that system 2 is lazy by design. The brain is a system that wants to work as efficiently as possible, which leads to you automatically wanting to trip over to system 1. Which makes sense why everyone is using AI to do their work for them, essentially you are in a state of constant conflict over what system your brain uses and system 1 usually wins.

I think it is good to let AI do some of the thinking for you, things like grammar and structure are often what takes up much of the content creation process and can be quite tedious. Where it's not a good idea is to tell AI to write an article about X, give no *context* and expect the correct answer. Without context AI is extremely useless, and arguably making you useless.

Having AI do the work for you is cognitive surrender.. leading you down a path of destruction.
